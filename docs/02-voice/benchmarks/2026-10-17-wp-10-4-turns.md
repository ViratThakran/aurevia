# WP-10.4 turn-handling benchmark (17 Oct 2026), partial

**These numbers use a canned model, not a real one.** It replies with fixed short lines, and
its first word comes after about 1.2 s (Gemini's measured first-token time). Everything else
is real: Deepgram nova-3 for speech to text, Cartesia for the agent's voice and for the
simulated prospect, and a local LiveKit 1.13.10 server. So the numbers **rank turn settings
against each other**. They are **not Gate 10 latency numbers**: a real model changes the
model part of every reply.

**Partial.** Cartesia ran out of credits (HTTP 402, `credits_exhausted`) at 10:51 UTC. Not
measured yet, **waiting on Cartesia credits**:
- D: runs 4 and 5 (only 6 of its calls completed);
- E: Deepgram key terms;
- F: preemptive text to speech.

No default was changed.

Raw data: [`2026-10-17-wp-10-4-turns.json`](2026-10-17-wp-10-4-turns.json).

## Method

- `python -m aurevia_voice.turn_benchmark` runs `aurevia_voice.simulate` 5 times per scenario
  for one worker setting, paced 5 s apart. It stops at the first failed call; there are no
  retries. The worker is restarted between settings.
- Scenarios:
  - `busy` and `skeptical`: existing simulator scripts;
  - `names`: new, the prospect says "Meera", "Aurevia" and "Priyanka Iyer".
- Each call reports the backend's latency summary. A number in the table is the **median
  across calls** of each call's p50 (or p95).
- "Reply time" is end of the prospect's speech to the agent's first audio, as the voice worker
  measures it.

(a) Not a voice failure. The local backend refused the benchmark's 11th login in 15 minutes
(its normal per-email login limit), so that call never started. The limit was raised on the
local test backend only.

(b) Almost all from the `names` scenario (25.8%). It is 0.8% on `busy` and 0.6% on
`skeptical`.

(c) One call ended after the greeting, and its cause was not captured: the runner kept only
the last error lines, which were LiveKit's harmless exit message. It now keeps the real lines.
The other failure was Cartesia's 402.

Model first word was 1226 to 1231 ms in every setting (the canned delay), so differences
between settings come from turn handling and speech.

## What this shows

- **Preemptive generation helps; keep it on.** Turning it off (B) costs about 340 ms at the
  median and about 1.1 s at p95.
- **Dynamic endpointing (C) is no better than fixed (A)** on these scripts.
- **A 0.8 s minimum wait (D)** adds about 225 ms of end-of-turn wait, but the median reply did
  not change (1668 ms against 1656 ms), because the model starts early either way. The worker's
  warning "transcript arrives after turn has been committed" appeared once each under A and B,
  and never under C or D. With only 6 calls this is a lead, not evidence; finish D first.
- **Names are badly heard without key terms, so E matters.** Across 5 `names` calls Deepgram
  never wrote "Aurevia" or "Meera" correctly:
  - "Meera" was "Mira" every time;
  - "Aurevia" was "Arabia", "Aryvia", "Oribia", "Auribia" or "Olivia";
  - "Priyanka" was right 4 of 5 times; "Iyer" was usually "Ayr".

  A prospect's "Is this Meera from Aurevia?" reaches the model as "Is this Mira from Arabia?".
